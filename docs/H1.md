## 2. Why this page is S&C only, and not the hitting lessons

The two source documents describe two different businesses:

- **`H1_Flyer_1.pdf`** — in-person HLP (High Level Pattern) hitting instruction.
  States plainly: *"We focus SOLELY on Leonardtown High School athletes, & do
  not venture out of our focus on this community,"* and *"the most
  cost-effective training program in Maryland exclusively for Leonardtown
  players."*
- **`H1_SC_UPDATES.pdf`** — the strength & conditioning program. References
  powerlifting PRs and 40-yard dash times, not just baseball, and is not
  location-bound.

The client's answers — all ages, all sports, remote-only — describe the second
document. So the page sells the S&C program, and the flyer supplies **coach
credentials only**.

**The client has since confirmed this explicitly: keep all hitting content off
this page.** That decision is settled, not a default. It means:

- No HLP (High Level Pattern) methodology, and no mention of Richard Schenck or
  of certified HLP instructor Austin Guy.
- No list of MLB hitters influenced by HLP's originator. That was a claim about
  a third party's client roster; on a company site it reads as an endorsement H1
  has not been given.
- **No "+15 MPH exit velocity" figure**, even though the S&C document also
  claims it. The flyer attributes that same number to the HLP hitting system, so
  it is entangled with the hitting program and does not belong on an S&C page.
  The results strip therefore carried three figures, not four.

  > **Amended 2026-08-04.** The owner asked for two hitting *outcome* metrics on
  > the page: **+20% exit velocity** and **+37% batted-ball distance**, both
  > averages across baseball athletes. Both now appear in `h1ProofPoints`, in the
  > hero readout and the results strip. The reasoning above is left standing
  > because it still describes why the figure was originally excluded, and
  > because it still governs everything else: this is a narrowing to outcome
  > metrics the owner supplied, **not** a reopening of hitting content generally.
  > No HLP methodology, no Richard Schenck or Austin Guy, no MLB roster claims,
  > no Leonardtown-exclusive framing. Note the excluded figure was +15 MPH from
  > the flyer; the +20% now on the page is a different, owner-supplied number.
- No Leonardtown-exclusive offering of any kind.

What stays is the coaches' **playing** record — batting lines, records, and the
two baseball photos. Those are athletic résumé and confirmed likeness, not a
hitting-instruction pitch, and stripping them would leave the page with no
credibility at all.

If hitting instruction is ever added, it needs its own page or a section scoped
explicitly to Leonardtown, so the "all ages, all sports, remote" promise above
it doesn't become false.

## 3. Proof, and what is not on this page

`h1Testimonials` in `data/h1.ts` drives the section. `H1TestimonialsSection`
has two states off that one array: while it is empty the section renders a
designed placeholder — a quote glyph, "The First Testimonies Are On The Way,"
and a prompt pointing current athletes at the form. Add an entry and the real
carousel replaces the placeholder with no other change.

The placeholder invents nothing: no sample quote, no fabricated name, no star
rating, no review count. It holds the space and tells the truth about why it is
empty.

> **Amended 2026-08-05.** The section is live. Mike Bittle (baseball, Class of
> 2027) and Colt Keen (baseball, Class of 2028) were supplied by the owner, each
> with a photo. The rules that govern every entry:
>
> - **Quotes are published verbatim as sent.** The placeholder promised
>   "posted the way they send them," and the grid keeps that promise. Results
>   quoted inside a testimonial (Mike's 82–97 mph exit velocity) are the
>   athlete's claim in the athlete's own words, attributed to the named
>   athlete — they do not graduate into `h1ProofPoints`, which remains
>   owner-published figures under the sourcing rules below.
> - **Hitting mentions inside a quote are fine.** An athlete describing their
>   own hitting outcomes is consistent with the §2 amendment (owner-supplied
>   outcome content); every §2 exclusion — HLP methodology, Richard Schenck,
>   Austin Guy, MLB roster claims, Leonardtown-exclusive framing — still
>   stands.
> - **Photos are optional.** `H1Testimonial.photo` is one `{src, alt}` field
>   so an image cannot ship without alt text. Files live under
>   `public/h1/testimonials/`, pre-cropped to the card's 4:3 header
>   (~1200px wide) so `object-cover` needs no per-photo tuning, and the card
>   masks them with the same bottom fade as the coach photography. A card
>   without a photo starts at the quote glyph.
> - **Minors.** Most H1 athletes are under 18. The owner was reminded on
>   2026-08-05 that a minor's name, photo, and words need a parent or
>   guardian's okay before publication; an entry arriving in `h1Testimonials`
>   means the owner has that handled.

> **Amended 2026-08-06.** Two more owner-supplied entries, each with a photo:
> Noah Earhart (baseball, Class of 2029) and Nick Hevner (baseball, college
> Class of 2029). The rules above are unchanged. Nick is 19 and said so in his
> quote, so the minors rule did not apply to his entry. Four cards now fill the
> wrapping row rather than a single tidy 3-up line; that is the flex-wrap
> layout doing its job, not a break.

> **Amended 2026-08-07.** Fifth entry: Alex Vasquez (baseball, Class of 2029),
> owner-supplied with a photo, rules above unchanged. His source photo was a
> tall phone-sized portrait, so the widest legal 4:3 crop out of it is
> **852x639** rather than the ~1200px the rule above describes. It is stored at
> that native size on purpose: upscaling to 1200px would have added file weight
> and no detail. 852px still covers the card, which never requests more than
> about 800px. Read the "~1200px wide" above as an aim, not a floor — the floor
> is a true 4:3 crop wide enough for the card.

> **Amended 2026-08-07 (layout).** The section is a carousel rather than a
> wrapping grid — owner's call, five quotes stacked down the page was too much
> to scroll past. **This supersedes the flex-wrap note in the 2026-08-06
> amendment above**; nothing about the content rules changes, and adding a
> testimony is still one `h1Testimonials` entry and nothing else.
>
> - **One row, three at a time.** Cards are 33.333%−1rem from `lg`, 50%−0.75rem
>   from `md`, full width below, so a whole number always fills the track and no
>   card peeks in half-shown at the right edge.
> - **Native scroller, not a JS slider.** `overflow-x: auto` with
>   `scroll-snap-type: x mandatory`, so a thumb or a trackpad gets the same
>   carousel the arrows give, and the section still renders its cards without
>   JavaScript. There is no carousel dependency and the page did not gain one.
> - **The arrows wrap.** Past the last page they return to the first, and back
>   from the first goes to the last, rather than disabling — on a list this
>   short a dead control is the worse of the two.
> - **They are measured, not assumed.** `canScroll` is a `ResizeObserver` read
>   of whether the track overflows; the arrows fade out when it does not (three
>   or fewer quotes on a desktop). It defaults to *true*: rendering the centred,
>   non-scrolling branch first puts the opening cards behind the scroll origin
>   where they cannot be reached, which on a phone opened the section on the
>   third testimony.
> - **Cards are all as tall as the longest quote**, which is what a single flex
>   row means. Nick Hevner's quote sets it — about 860px desktop, 880px mobile —
>   leaving 90–180px under the shorter quotes. The attributions are pinned to
>   the card bottom, so that space reads as a column of aligned names rather
>   than as four ragged cards. If the spread ever gets uncomfortable, the fix is
>   to clamp the quote with a "read more" expander, not to unstretch the cards:
>   the track's height is the tallest card either way, and an unstretched short
>   card just floats above the section background.

> **Amended 2026-08-07 (loop + peek).** Owner's call: "a true circle through
> them and show theres more like faded beside them." This supersedes exactly two
> bullets of the layout amendment above — "no card peeks in half-shown at the
> right edge" and "the arrows wrap … past the last page they return to the
> first" — and nothing else. Adding a testimony is still one `h1Testimonials`
> entry and nothing else.
>
> - **It is a real loop now.** The five cards are flanked, *on the client only*,
>   by two cloned copies, and the scroller is silently re-banded into the middle
>   copy when a press starts and when the reader's own scrolling settles. Going
>   forward past the fifth testimony continues into the first with no visible
>   return scroll: measured, the animated travel is one card on every press
>   including the wrap, and the re-band is a single frame between pixel-identical
>   positions. Server HTML still contains exactly five `<figure>` elements, so
>   crawlers and no-JS readers get the real list once.
> - **The loop period must be measured, never `scrollWidth / 3`.** Fifteen cards
>   have fourteen gaps, not fifteen, and `scrollWidth` also carries the two peek
>   gutters, so that shortcut is one whole gap long — 2314px against a true
>   2290px. Mandatory snapping absorbs the error, which is what makes it
>   dangerous: the loop still appears to work while every seam re-settles
>   visibly. Take the difference between the same card in adjacent copies.
> - **Neighbours peek through a fade.** The track carries a `--peek` gutter
>   (2rem, 3rem from `md`) with matching `scroll-padding`, so a slice of the
>   previous and next card shows at each edge under a gradient painted in the
>   section's own steel. Visible slice = peek − gap: 24px from `md`, 16px below,
>   where the gap drops to 1rem so the slice does not shrink to an 8px artifact.
>   **The card width classes are unchanged** — a flex percentage resolves against
>   the content box, which the gutter has already narrowed, so the same three
>   classes that used to meet the track's edges now leave the peek instead.
> - **The fades are painted over the track, not masked out of it.** The
>   coaches-section argument for masking does not transfer: the surface here is
>   flat `steel`, so overlay and mask are the same pixels. What differs is that a
>   `mask-image` on the focusable track erases its focus ring outright — measured
>   at zero surviving ring pixels against 2864 on an unmasked control. The
>   explicit `#h1-testimonials-track:focus-visible` rule in `globals.css` exists
>   for the same reason.
> - **Looping is measured, not assumed**, like `canScroll`, and gated harder: it
>   engages only when the real list overflows by at least one whole card. Below
>   that — possible if the list shrinks to four — a card and its own clone could
>   be on screen together, the same face at both edges. There the arrows fall
>   back to the previous long-scroll wrap, which is worse than looping but far
>   better than a dead control.
> - Every measurement (stride, period, gap, gutter) is read from the DOM at the
>   point of use. No pixel figure in the component is load-bearing, and none
>   should be copied into this document as one.

The results strip and the hero readout carry six figures, from two sources. Two
are quoted verbatim from the client's own S&C document; four were supplied by
the owner on 2026-08-04 and are published nowhere but this page:

| Figure | Scope | Source claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1 → 4.5 40-yard dash | individual | "A 40-yard dash from 5.1 to 4.5 in 2 months" |
| 7.2 → 6.6 60-yard dash | individual | Owner, 2026-08-04 |
| 120 → 235 lb bench | individual | Owner, 2026-08-04 |
| +30% powerlifting PR | individual | "increased PR numbers by 30% in ONE month" |
| +37% batted-ball distance | **average** | Owner, 2026-08-04 — see §2 amendment |
| +20% exit velocity | **average** | Owner, 2026-08-04 — see §2 amendment |

"4-week blocks" is not in this table. It is a program attribute, not a measured
result, so it lives in `h1BlockNote` and `h1ProgramFeatures` rather than in
`h1ProofPoints`.

The S&C document's "+15 MPH exit velocity" figure remains excluded — the +20%
above is a different number, supplied by the owner.

**Four of these are one athlete; two are averages.** That distinction is stored
once, in `H1ProofPoint.scope`, and turned into words in exactly one place —
`proofAttribution()` in `data/h1.ts`.

Two rules follow, and they are rules, not descriptions:

- **Nothing may render a figure without also rendering its attribution.** That
  holds in the hero readout and the results strip alike.
- **Do not add a second field describing scope.** Two fields can disagree, and
  the disagreement here would be an average presented as one athlete's result.

The owner's bench figure arrived without a unit — "120 to 235". Pounds was
confirmed by the owner on 2026-08-04.

The sourcing rule is unchanged. Do not round these, restate them as typical, or
add figures without a source. `5.1 → 4.5` is the published precision; rendering
it as `5.10 → 4.50` would be inventing a decimal place the client never claimed.

**The hero readout counts every figure as it arrives** (changed 2026-08-06; it
previously animated only the three pairs). A pair counts between its two
published numbers. A delta has no baseline to count from, so it fills from zero
— `+0%` climbing to `+30%` — which reads as a meter filling rather than as a
claim that the athlete started at nothing. Two properties keep that inside the
sourcing rule above, and both are load-bearing: the count's target and its
decimal places are read out of the published string itself rather than stored
beside it, so `+30%` can neither count to `+35%` nor land on `+30.0%`; and the
readout always comes to rest on the published string, never on an in-between
value. Nothing mid-count is ever announced — the readout is `aria-hidden`, and
the settled, attributed set lives in the results strip.

No ratings and no review counts appear in the structured data, for the same
reason.

## 4. Brand

`VENTURE_COLORS.h1` in the Lee Enterprises Unlimited repo's `lib/colors.ts` is
gold-on-black, close enough to the LEU parent gold and to LMM's amber that the
family of sites reads as one brand. It styles the H1 card on the parent
homepage and is not in this repo; if `gold` below ever changes, change it there
too.

`H1_PALETTE` in `data/h1.ts` is the palette for everything here:

| Token | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ink` | `#08090A` | Page background |
| `steel` | `#12141A` | Alternating section bands |
| `chalk` | `#F5F7FA` | Primary type, primary buttons. Always with `ink` text |
| `gold` | `#FFD700` | Badges, stat figures, icon strokes, accents |
| `goldDeep` | `#E0AC00` | Gradient ends, hovers |
| `line` | `#23262E` | Card borders and hairlines |
| `muted` | `#8C93A1` | Secondary type |

One documented exception: `.h1c-hero-bg` in `globals.css` uses `#0c0e12` and
`#050607`, a shade either side of `ink`, so the hero background has a centre.
They are vignette values rather than brand colours and appear nowhere else.
Every brand colour in the stylesheet arrives as a `--h1c-*` custom property,
set on `<html>` by `app/layout.tsx` from `H1_PALETTE`, so editing the palette
cannot leave a stale colour behind. The one qualifier: the `border-color` and
`outline-color` rules in `@layer base` carry literal fallbacks —
`var(--h1c-line, #23262E)`, `var(--h1c-gold, #FFD700)` — for the instant before
the inline style hydrates. They match the palette exactly, so a palette edit
that changed those two values would want the fallbacks changed with it; nothing
else in the stylesheet does.

H1's own mark is white on black, so this palette leads with chalk white and
keeps gold as an accent. That inverts LMM's amber-dominant look while staying
true to the logo.

## 5. Wiring on the parent site

Everything that reaches this site from `leeenterprisesunlimited.com` lives in
the Lee Enterprises Unlimited repo, and all of it resolves to one value: that
deployment's `NEXT_PUBLIC_H1_SITE_URL` environment variable (`config/h1-site.mjs`
and `lib/h1-site.ts` there). Set it to this site's domain and the three below
follow.

1. `next.config.mjs` redirects `/h1-performance`, and anything under it, here
   permanently, so old links and search results carry over.
2. `data/navigation.ts` — the H1 entry in `ventureNavItems` and `venturePages`
   is this site's URL. `MainHeader`'s `handleNavClick` treats an absolute URL
   the way it treats a route: a full navigation, not a scroll.
3. `data/websites.ts` — H1 stays in `websitePortfolio`, the list of LEU's own
   sites shown as proof on the LMM websites page, linked at its new address.

The parent keeps its own copy of `public/logos/h1-performance-white.png` because
`data/ventures.ts` there still names it; nothing on the parent renders that
field today (its header, footer and hero use the LEU mark), so the file has no
current consumer on that side. This repo carries the mark for its own use.

## 6. Lead capture

`POST /api/h1-consultation` — its own route rather than a flag on a shared one,
because the subject line is what distinguishes an H1 lead in the inbox:

```
New H1 Consultation Request — {sport} — {athlete or requester}
```

Recipient resolves `H1_TO_EMAIL || CONTACT_TO_EMAIL || ceo@…`. `H1_TO_EMAIL` is
documented but unset, so requests land in the same Lee Enterprises inbox as the
parent site's forms — the intended behavior. It carries the same caveat as
every form on the parent site: until a domain is verified in Resend, any
recipient other than the account's own address is refused and the form silently
fails. The variables are set in this project's Vercel environment, not the
parent's.

The form asks for requester **and** athlete separately, because the person
filling it in is often a parent. Age/grade, sport, and available equipment are
captured up front since the program is built around the athlete's actual setup.
Honeypot, server-side validation, and field-level error mapping match the forms
on the parent site.

### Minors

The offer is open to all ages, so a meaningful share of leads will be about
someone under 18, and the page sells nutrition and supplement guidance.
`H1_NUTRITION_DISCLAIMER` renders beneath the pricing section: educational, not
medical advice, consult a physician, guardian involved for under-18s. Keep it on
the page if the nutrition or supplement copy stays.

## 9. The redesign (2026-10-24)

The owner asked for a full upgrade of the site, modelled on the most
decorated baseball training company there is. Every rule in §1–§3 still
governs every figure, quote, and credential. What changed is how they are
presented. Nothing was added to the claims.

### The benchmark

Research on 2026-10-24 compared the main contenders: Driveline Baseball,
Tread Athletics, Cressey Sports Performance, Florida Baseball Ranch, Baseball
Rebellion, and IMG Academy.

- **Driveline** is the clear leader by recognition. It was an Inc. 5000
  honoree. It reports 100+ MLB players and over 1,000 pro players trained.
  More than 50 of its former staff have been hired across pro baseball.
- **Tread Athletics** is the closest operational match for a fully remote
  program, and has the largest YouTube audience.

The site borrows Driveline's proof system and Tread's remote-funnel mechanics:

- a proof bar directly under the hero;
- a "plan, tools, coach" value stack;
- an assess → train → re-test loop;
- the same CTA pair repeated down the page;
- a plain, coach-like voice;
- an athlete-or-parent choice up front.

The visual language follows award-winning sports sites: a condensed display
face in caps, a mono "telemetry" voice for labels, and data-instrument motifs.

**What was deliberately not borrowed:** brag-bar counts (MLB players, draft
picks, All-Stars). H1 has no such figures, and §3 forbids inventing them. The
six sourced results in `h1ProofPoints` are the only figures on the page.

### Design system

- **Type.** Three families, set in `app/layout.tsx` and mapped in the
  `@theme inline` block of `globals.css`:
  - Barlow Condensed (600/700/800, plus 800 italic) for display. It has
    tabular figures, so counting numbers do not jitter.
  - Geist for body text.
  - Geist Mono for labels and indexes (`.h1-eyebrow`).
- **Palette.** One token was added: `slate` (`#4A505C`), secondary type on
  `chalk`. `muted` drops to about 3:1 on the one light section; `slate` holds
  about 8:1. It is set as `--h1c-slate` like every other token.
- **Texture.** All decorative and `aria-hidden`:
  - `.h1-grain`: a static SVG-noise layer over the page.
  - `.h1-grid-bg`: a fading lab grid.
  - `.h1-hud`: corner ticks.
  - `.h1-seam`: baseball-stitch borders.
  - `.h1-outline-text`: outlined background wordmarks.
- **Motion.**
  - Hero elements enter once on load (`.h1-enter`), under a second, never
    repeated. The `<h1>` itself never starts hidden: it is the likely LCP
    element.
  - Everything else is CSS scroll-driven (`animation-timeline: view()` /
    `scroll()`): reveals (`.rv`), bars (`.rv-grow-x/y`), chart strokes
    (`.rv-draw`), the diamond's base path, the ticker, and the header's
    reading-progress line. Motion the reader drives with their own scrolling
    stops when they stop, so the ticker needs no pause button. The hero
    readout, which runs on a clock, keeps its pause control.
  - All of it sits behind `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, and the
    scroll timelines also sit behind `@supports`. A browser without support
    shows everything at rest.
- **The `overflow-clip` rule.** `view()` measures against the nearest scroll
  container, and `overflow: hidden` makes one. Inside an `overflow-hidden`
  section every reveal froze. Nothing that wraps an animated element may use
  `overflow-hidden`; the components use `overflow-clip`, which clips
  identically.
- **Hover vocabulary.** `.h1-tap` and `.h1-live` are unchanged. Three
  additions:
  - `.h1-btn-gold`: the primary button, with a light sweep.
  - `.h1-card`: the coach cards, with a lift and a sheen on hover only.
  - `.h1-on-gold`: switches focus rings to ink on the gold band, where a gold
    ring would vanish.

### Derived displays — and why each is within the rules

- **`proofChange()`** (the Δ chip on a pair, e.g. `−0.6`, `+115 lb`). This is
  arithmetic on the two published figures, at their published precision. It
  is not a new claim and not a percentage, which would introduce rounding.
- **`proofMagnitudes()`** (the charts):
  - A pair plots its own two figures on a zero-based scale.
  - A percentage plots 100 against 100 + the figure, which is all "+30%"
    states.
  - The spray chart's arcs are in exactly the 1 : 1.37 ratio "+37%" gives.
    The dial's baseline tick sits at 100/120.
  - No axis values, no invented intermediate points, no "batted balls".
  - The results footnote says the charts show only ratios.
- **The block staircase** is labelled "Illustration." It shows only that
  blocks build on each other: no values, no shape within a block.
- **`H1Coach.featured`** (the stat strip) may only restate lines already in
  `credentials`. `.360` and `.934` restate "Career at St. Mary's — .360 AVG,
  .934 OPS". "2024 / 4A State Champion" restates "2024 4A State Champion",
  and so on.
- **`H1Testimonial.highlight`** is one sentence set apart in each card.
  - It must be an exact substring of the quote. The card checks this at
    render time; a highlight that is not found renders the quote plain, so a
    paraphrase can never be shown as something said.
  - Emphasis inside a verbatim quote does not graduate a result into
    `h1ProofPoints`: Mike's and Noah's exit-velocity lines remain their claims
    in their words, attributed to them (§3).
- **"No payment to book" / "Free consultation first"** under pricing restate
  §1 ("This page books consultations, it does not sell"). There is
  still no cancellation or contract claim beyond the existing "no long-term
  contract to sign before you know it fits".

### Behaviour that is new

- **Package pre-selection.** `goToConsultation(name)` in
  `lib/consultation.ts` fires `h1:choose-package`. The form accepts the name
  only if it is one of its own options. The form section now takes focus
  (`tabIndex={-1}`); before this, the scroll helper's `focus: true` on a
  plain section did nothing.
- **Athlete or parent.**
  - A radio group at the top of the form, styled as a segmented control,
    changes labels and helper text.
  - "Parent" makes the athlete's name required, on the client and in
    `app/api/h1-consultation/route.ts`.
  - The route accepts `requesterRole` (`athlete` | `parent`) and writes it
    into the email's "Requested By" line.
  - An unknown or missing value is ignored, not rejected, so an older cached
    page still submits. The lookup uses `Object.hasOwn`, so `"constructor"`
    is ignored rather than matched.
- **`H1StickyCTA`.** A phone-only booking bar.
  - It hides while the hero, the form, or the closing band is on screen; those
    three carry `data-sticky-cta="hide"`.
  - Hidden, it is `inert`.
  - The footer carries matching bottom padding below `md`.
- **The link preview** (`app/opengraph-image.tsx`) is now the hero in one
  frame, set in Barlow Condensed with Hunter's cutout. `next/og` can use
  neither next/font's files nor WebP, so `assets/og/` carries:
  - the two TTFs, with their SIL OFL 1.1 text in `assets/og/OFL.txt`, as the
    licence requires;
  - a 600px PNG of the cutout.

  `lib/og.ts` reads them. `assets/` is outside `public/`, so nothing there is
  served to visitors.
